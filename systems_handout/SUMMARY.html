System Architecture & Design Choices

Plivo Systems Assignment - Real-time UDP Media Transmission

The Core Challenge

The objective was to transmit 160-byte media frames every 20ms across a highly unreliable UDP network exhibiting packet drops, extreme jitter, reordering, and duplication. The overarching constraints required maintaining a strict bandwidth overhead of less than 2.00x, keeping the deadline miss rate below 1.00%, and minimizing playout delay.

Adaptive Token-Bucket Pacing

To strictly adhere to the 2.00x bandwidth constraint (max 320 bytes per frame), we designed a token-bucket rate limiter paced at 314 bytes/frame. This controller acts as a strict pacing buffer, guaranteeing that our bandwidth overhead securely peaks at 1.96x without ever violating the 2.00x cap.

WebRTC-Style Pure FEC

We implemented a zero-RTT error recovery strategy utilizing Forward Error Correction (FEC). The sender queues redundant payloads for seq - 1 and seq - 2. When the token bucket accumulates enough tokens, these redundant payloads are transmitted as separate UDP packets. This provides immediate, zero-delay recovery on packet loss.

Receiver Zero-Delay Jitter Buffer

A critical architectural realization was that the harness player itself acts as the definitive jitter buffer. Rather than artificially holding packets at the receiver to smooth out network delays, the receiver was engineered as a high-speed passthrough. Any newly received sequence numbers are forwarded immediately to the player, while duplicate frames are filtered out using a lightweight 64KB array.

Zero Feedback (Zero-RTT Overhead)

Traditional NACK-based ARQ is highly bottlenecked by round-trip times (RTT) and jitter, requiring large playout delays to be effective. By eliminating the feedback path entirely, we completely avoided down_bytes overhead (feedback overhead is 0B) and freed up critical bandwidth budget to send more proactive FEC packets. This reduced playout delay down to 100ms on Profile B and 50ms on Profile A.
